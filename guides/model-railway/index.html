How to catalogue a model railway collection

Updated 11 November 2026 · Free template included

Locomotives, rolling stock, a layout that keeps growing. A model railway is one of the easiest collections to lose track of, because the pieces look alike on the shelf and the details that matter, gauge, running number, livery, are exactly the ones you forget. Here is a simple way to get it all down, with a free spreadsheet to start today.

What to record for each piece

  • Make and part number. Hornby, Bachmann, Lima and the catalogue number, which is how you find a spare or a match later.
  • Gauge. OO, HO, N and so on. Record it on everything, because a mixed gauge collection is easy to muddle.
  • Railway and livery. BR, GWR, LMS, and the colour scheme. Livery drives both value and whether a piece fits your era.
  • Class, wheel arrangement, loco name and running number. The details that tell two near identical models apart.
  • Era or year the model represents, and whether you have the original box.
  • What you paid, the condition, and the current value if you track it.

A method that scales

  • Work by storage box or shelf. Log one box at a time rather than the whole layout at once.
  • Use the menus. The template has ready made lists for make, type, gauge, railway and livery, so your data stays consistent enough to sort and filter.
  • Note the running number. It is the single most useful field for telling two of the same class apart, and the easiest to forget.
  • Photograph the loco and its box. Boxed condition matters for value, and a photo of the base often carries the part number.

Keeping track of what it is worth

A boxed, mint locomotive and a well run one of the same class can be worth very different amounts, and a spreadsheet makes you track that by hand. For a small collection that is fine. For a serious one, value is the first thing that turns a spreadsheet into a chore, and a collection worth real money is worth documenting properly for insurance.
